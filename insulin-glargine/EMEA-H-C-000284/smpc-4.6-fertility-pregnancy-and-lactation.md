# 4.6 Pregnancy and lactation

For insulin glargine no clinical data on exposed pregnancies are available.
Animal studies do not indicate direct or indirect harmful effects with respect to pregnancy, embryonal / foetal development, parturition or postnatal development (see 5.3).
Caution should be exercised when prescribing to pregnant women.
It is essential for patients with pre-existing or gestational diabetes to maintain good metabolic control throughout pregnancy.
Insulin requirements may decrease during the first trimester and generally increase during the second and third trimesters.
Immediately after delivery, insulin requirements decline rapidly (increased risk of hypoglycaemia).
Careful monitoring of glucose control is essential.

Lactating women may require adjustments in insulin dose and diet.
