# 5.2 Pharmacokinetic properties

Pharmacokinetic parameters were assessed in patients with APL (n=12), advanced haematologic malignancies (n=12), and advanced solid tumours (n=22) who participated in one of three TRISENOX clinical trials after single- and multiple intravenous doses of 0.06 to 0.30 mg/kg administered daily.
In these studies, plasma concentrations of total arsenic only were measured and analysed.
The pharmacokinetics of trivalent arsenic, the active species of TRISENOX, have not been characterised.

The extent of exposure (as reflected by the AUC, Area Under the Curve) to arsenic appeared to increase proportionately with increasing dose.
Plasma concentrations of arsenic were maximal near the end of the infusion and declined slowly thereafter.
With daily dosing, plasma concentrations appeared to achieve steady-state levels in 8 to 10 days.

Metabolism: Arsenic is stored mainly in liver, kidney, heart, lung, hair and nails.
Trivalent forms of arsenic are methylated in humans and mostly excreted in urine.
In APL patients, daily administration of 0.15 mg/kg/day of arsenic trioxide resulted in an approximate fourfold increase in the urinary excretion of arsenic after 2 to 4 weeks of continuous dosing, when compared to baseline values.

Excretion: Studies with radiolabelled arsenic trioxide have demonstrated that after oral administration of 0.06 ng arsenic, approximately 60% of the radioactivity was recovered in the urine within 8 days.
The mean plasma elimination t1/2 value in patients receiving TRISENOX was 92 hours.
This 92-hour plasma elimination half-life is consistent with the reported 3 to 5 day urinary excretion half-life for arsenic.
