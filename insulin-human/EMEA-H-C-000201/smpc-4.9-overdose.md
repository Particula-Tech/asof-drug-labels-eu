# 4.9 Overdose

Symptoms

Insulin overdose may lead to severe and sometimes long-term and life-threatening hypoglycaemia.

Management

Patients who are still conscious must immediately take 10 g to 20 g of glucose (or the equivalent amount of another sugar) and repeat this if necessary approximately every 20 minutes.
Additionally, long-acting carbohydrates must be administered.
Where the patient is unconscious, an intramuscular injection of 1 mg of glucagon is given.
There is no relevant risk as compared to the expected benefit in injecting glucagon even when hypoglycaemia is uncertain.
Glucagon is, however, not recommended following heavy alcohol consumption (no adequate glucagon response to be expected).
As an alternative to glucagon, or if the glucagon injection fails to restore metabolic control immediately, adults may receive 20 ml to 30 ml of a 30% to 50% glucose solution intravenously, and this dose may be repeated if necessary.
In children, the dose is reduced in proportion to body weight.

In case of severe or prolonged hypoglycaemia, it is generally necessary that glucagon injection and/or acute glucose administration is followed up by infusion of a less concentrated glucose solution (while monitoring the blood glucose level) to prevent recurrence of hypoglycaemia.
In addition, the administration of glucagon (intramuscular or subcutaneous) may be repeated.
In young children, because of the possible risk of dangerous hyperglycaemia, glucose must be administered with extreme caution while monitoring blood glucose levels very closely.
Once the patient has regained consciousness, long-acting carbohydrates are given orally to prevent a relapse into hypoglycaemia.

Careful monitoring is required until the patient can be assumed to be out of danger.
It must be remembered that hypoglycaemia may recur after initial recovery, and that the subjective warning signs of subsequent hypoglycaemia may be changed, weakened or absent.
Hospitalisation (even as a precautionary measure) may be required in certain circumstances.
Monitoring and treatment in the intensive care unit may become necessary.
