# 4.6 Use during pregnancy and lactation

Pregnancy

There is no experience with the use of Insuman Rapid in pregnant women.

If untreated during pregnancy, diabetes mellitus constitutes a risk in intrauterine development.
Diabetes therapy must therefore be continued during pregnancy.

Insulin does not cross the placental barrier.
Both hypoglycaemia and hyperglycaemia increase the risk of malformations and death in utero.
Hyperglycaemia increases the risk of macrosomy (the latter leading to an increased risk of dystocia).
Particularly careful glycaemic control and metabolic monitoring are therefore essential throughout pregnancy, and patients must inform their physician if they plan to become or have become pregnant.
Insulin requirements change during pregnancy and necessitate dose adjustment.
Requirements fall during the first trimester of pregnancy, and are increased during the later stages (generally starting in the second trimester).
Immediately after delivery, insulin requirements decline rapidly (increased risk of hypoglycaemia).

Lactation

There are no restrictions on the use of Insuman Rapid in breast-feeding women.
Lactating women may require adjustments in insulin dose and diet.
