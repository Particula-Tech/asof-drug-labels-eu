# 4.2 Posology and method of administration

Principles

The dosage should be determined by the physician according to the requirement of the patient.
The desired blood glucose levels, the insulin preparations to be used and the insulin dosage (doses and timings) must be determined individually and adjusted to suit the patient’s diet, physical activity and life-style.
The risk of hypoglycaemia must be taken into account if normal blood glucose levels are desired.
Insulin therapy generally requires appropriate patient education and self-monitoring.
It must be decided on an individual basis whether and to what extent patients are able responsibly to manage their own insulin treatment and to monitor glucose levels

Individual insulin doses are based on the results of blood glucose measurements as well as on the patient's scheduled physical activity and carbohydrate intake.
The physician gives the patient guidance on the frequency and timing of blood glucose measurements, as well as on whether, how often and when urine tests for glucose and ketones must be performed.
In addition, it is recommended that the quality of metabolic control be checked by regular determinations of glycated haemoglobin (see also under 4.4).

With intramuscular and subcutaneous insulin therapy, it must be noted that insulin absorption is subject to physiological and external factors (e.g. tissue perfusion, physical exercise, temperature).
Patients must be given guidance on what to do, for example, if they fail to keep to the prescribed dosage regimen, if they inadvertently take too high an insulin dose or miss an insulin dose entirely, or if they have to skip meals or cannot eat at the prescribed times.

Daily doses and timing of administration

There are no fixed rules for insulin dosage.
However, the average insulin requirement is often 0.5 to 1.0 IU per kg body weight per day.
The basal metabolic requirement is 40% to 60% of the total daily requirement.
In general, Insuman Rapid is injected subcutaneously 15 to 20 minutes before a meal.

Transfer to Insuman Rapid

Dosage adjustment may be necessary when transferring patients from one insulin preparation to another.
This applies, for example, when transferring from:

- an animal insulin (especially a bovine insulin) to human insulin (the main reason for this is the lower affinity of human insulin for insulin antibodies; it is also thought that human insulin is absorbed more rapidly from the injection depot).
- one human insulin formulation to another.
- a regimen with only regular insulin to one with a longer-acting insulin.

The need to adjust (e.g. reduce) the dose may become evident immediately after transfer.
Alternatively, it may emerge gradually over a period of several weeks.

Following transfer from an animal insulin to human insulin, dosage reduction may be required in particular in patients who

- were previously already controlled on rather low blood glucose levels.
- have a tendency to hypoglycaemia.
- previously required high insulin doses due to the presence of insulin antibodies.

A programme of particularly frequent metabolic monitoring is necessary during transfer and in the initial weeks thereafter.
In patients who require high insulin doses because of the presence of insulin antibodies, transfer under medical supervision in a hospital or similar setting must be considered.

Secondary dose adjustment

Improved metabolic control may result in increased insulin sensitivity, leading to a reduced insulin requirement.
Dose adjustment may also be required, for example, if

- the patient's weight changes.
- the patient's life-style changes.
- other circumstances arise that may promote an increased susceptibility to hypo- or hyperglycaemia (see under 4.4).

The dose may also have to be adjusted during intercurrent illness (see 4.4).

Administration

Insuman Rapid contains 100 IU of insulin per ml solution.
Only syringes designed for this strength of insulin (100 IU per ml) are to be used.
The syringes must not contain any other medicinal product or residue (e.g. traces of heparin).
Insuman Rapid is administered by deep subcutaneous injection.
However, in principle, it may also be administered by intramuscular injection.
The onset of action is slightly faster and the duration of action slightly shorter after intramuscular injection than after subcutaneous injection.
Insulin does not have a sufficient blood glucose lowering effect if taken orally.
Insulin absorption and hence the blood glucose lowering effect of a dose may vary from one injection area to another (e.g. the abdominal wall compared with the thigh).
Patients must therefore change the injection area only after consulting their physician.
However, injection sites within an injection area must be rotated from one injection to the next.

Mixing of insulins

Insuman Rapid may be mixed with all Hoechst human insulins, but NOT with those designed specifically for use in insulin pumps.
Insuman Rapid must also NOT be mixed with insulins of animal origin.

[Insuman Infusat 100 IU/ml solution for injection in a vial.]

Insuman Infusat has been specially designed for use in external portable insulin pumps.
It is specially stabilised to minimise loss of efficacy which may result from mechanical and thermal stress in such pumps.
Insuman Infusat is therefore also suitable for continuous insulin infusion with other, conventional syringe pumps.
The human insulin in Insuman Infusat is produced by recombinant DNA technology using K 12 strains of Escherichia coli.

The desired blood glucose levels and the insulin dosage must be determined individually and adjusted to suit the patient's diet, physical activity and life-style.

Dosage and dose distribution throughout the day are based on the results of blood glucose measurements as well as on the patient's scheduled physical activity and carbohydrate intake.

With subcutaneous insulin therapy, it must be noted that insulin absorption is subject to physiological and external factors (e.g. tissue perfusion, physical exercise, temperature).

When used in external portable insulin pumps, part of the daily insulin dose is infused continuously ("basal rate"), and the rest is administered in the form of bolus injections before meals.
Refer to the operating instructions for detailed information about the infusion pump, its functions and the necessary safety precautions.

Consequently, about 40% to 60% of the daily dose is administered at a basal rate, and the rest is given as bolus injections before meals.

Transfer to Insuman Infusat

Insuman Infusat may be infused by the subcutaneous route.
It is designed for use in the ®Hoechst Infusor and ®H-Tron insulin pumps.
It may also be used in other insulin pumps for which it has been shown that they are suitable for this insulin (see pump manual).
Only tetrafluoroethylene or polyethylene catheters must be used.
Insuman Infusat must not be used in peristaltic pumps with silicone tubing.
Insulin must always be infused under aseptic conditions.
This is facilitated by the special equipment available for insulin pumps (e.g. catheters, cannulas).
The puncture site within a given injection area must be changed regularly (generally, every 1 to 3 days).
If the infusion pump malfunctions, Insuman Infusat may be drawn up into a syringe and injected.
However, it must be noted that Insuman Infusat is a highly concentrated insulin containing 100 IU insulin per ml solution.

Insuman Infusat must NOT be mixed with any other insulins.

[Insuman Infusat 100 IU/ml solution for injection in a cartridge.]

Insuman Infusat in cartridges may be infused by the subcutaneous route.
It is designed for use in the ®Hoechst Infusor and ®H Tron insulin pumps.
It may also be used in other insulin pumps for which it has been shown that they are suitable for this insulin and this type of cartridge (see pump manual).
This is facilitated by the special equipment available for the insulin pumps (e.g. catheters, cannulas).
