# 5.3 Preclinical safety data

Secondary pharmacology
The potential effect of travoprost on blood flow to the optic nerve head was evaluated in rabbits, since reduced blood flow may be a component in the aetiology of glaucoma.
In contrast, travoprost significantly increased optic nerve head blood flow in rabbits following 7 days of topical ocular administration (1.4 micrograms, once daily).

No changes in retinal function as assessed by electroretinogram were produced by a single subcutaneous dose (100 micrograms/kg) of travoprost to rabbits when measured at either one hour or one week after dosing.

Safety data
The ocular as well as systemic toxicity of travoprost has been investigated in several animal species.
Generally, travoprost is well tolerated with a safety margin between clinical ocular dose and systemic toxicity of at least 250 times.

Single dose toxicity is low, with no significant pharmacotoxic observations in rats at intravenous dose of 10 mg/kg, which is at least 250,000 times the clinical dose, and over 13,000 times the possible exposure from the entire contents of one product container.
A six-month toxicity study in rats by subcutaneous administration demonstrated trace-to-mild hyperostosis and/or endosteal fibrosis observed at 30 and 100 micrograms/kg, with a no-effect level of 10 micrograms/kg/day (200 times the clinical dose).
Similar findings were present at 10 micrograms/kg/day following two years of treatment in rats.
Topical ocular administration of travoprost to monkeys at concentrations of up to 0.012% to the right eye, twice daily for one year resulted in no systemic toxicity.

Reproduction toxicity studies have been undertaken in rat, mice and rabbit by systemic route.
Findings are related to FP receptor agonist activity in uterus with early embryolethality, post-implantation loss, foetotoxicity.
In pregnant rat, systemic administration of travoprost at doses more than 200 times the clinical dose during the period of organogenesis resulted in an increased incidence of malformations.
Low levels of radioactivity were measured in amniotic fluid and foetal tissues of pregnant rats administered 3H-travoprost.
Reproduction and development studies have demonstrated a potent effect on foetal loss with a high rate observed in rats and mice (180 pg/ml and 30 pg/ml plasma, respectively) at exposures 1.2 to 6 times the clinical exposure (up to 25 pg/ml).

In ocular toxicity studies in monkeys, administration of travoprost at a dose of 0.45 microgram, twice a day, was shown to induce increased palpebral fissure.

Carcinogenicity
Two year bioassays, in which rats and mice were dosed with travoprost by subcutaneous injection at doses up to 100 micrograms/kg/day (2,500 times the clinical dose), revealed no evidence of carcinogenic effect.
Occasional significance of some statistical tumour analyses were observed, but were considered to be incidental, and not related to treatment with travoprost.
Mutagenicity
Travoprost was not mutagenic in bacteria, in one mouse lymphoma assay, in the mouse micronucleus tests nor in the rat chromosome aberration assay.
In another mouse lymphoma assay, mutagenicity was equivocal in one of two replicates in the presence, but not in the absence, of activation enzymes.
