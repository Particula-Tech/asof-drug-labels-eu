# 4.5 Interaction with other medicinal products and other forms of interaction

Serious Drug Interactions

Co-administration of colchicine with strong P-gp inhibitors and/or strong CYP3A4 inhibitors will increase exposure to colchicine, which may lead to colchicine-induced toxicity, including fatalities.
Such concomitant use is contraindicated (see section 4.3, and section 4.5, Table 1).

Colchicine has not been studied for drug-drug interactions, however data and information available in the public domain related to the drug interaction potential of colchicine are presented.
Colchicine is a substrate of the efflux transporter P-glycoprotein (P-gp).
Of the cytochrome P450 enzymes tested, CYP3A4 was mainly involved in the metabolism of colchicine.
If colchicine is administered with medicinal products that inhibit P-gp, most of which also inhibit CYP3A4, increased concentrations of colchicine are likely.

Physicians should ensure that patients are suitable candidates for treatment with colchicine and remain alert for signs and symptoms of toxicities related to increased colchicine exposure as a result of a drug interaction.
Signs and symptoms of colchicine toxicity should be evaluated promptly and, if toxicity is suspected, consider interruption or discontinuation of colchicine.

Interactions with food and drink

Patients are advised to avoid the use of grapefruit or grapefruit juice with colchicine, as it leads to increased exposure to colchicine.

Oral contraceptives

Colchicine can interact with oral contraceptives like norethindrone/ethinyl estradiol and can cause adverse reactions like diarrhoea, nausea, upper abdominal pain, cold sweat etc. Females using oral contraceptives should be prescribed colchicine with caution and observed for adverse reactions.
