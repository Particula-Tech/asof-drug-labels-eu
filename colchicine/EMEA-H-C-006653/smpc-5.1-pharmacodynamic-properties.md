# 5.1 Pharmacodynamic properties

Pharmacotherapeutic group: Antigout preparations, preparations with no effect on uric acid metabolism, ATC code: M04AC01

Mechanism of action

The mechanism of action of colchicine in the prevention of major cardiovascular (CV) events is not understood.
Colchicine is considered to act against the inflammatory response by possibly inhibiting the migration of granulocytes to the inflamed area and by interaction with the microtubules, which together could contribute to the prevention of CV events.

Colchicine is an anti-inflammatory medicinal product that inhibits microtubule polymerisation and modulates inflammatory pathways implicated in atherosclerosis, including neutrophil activation and the NLRP3 inflammasome.
Through these mechanisms, colchicine reduces vascular inflammation, which contributes to residual cardiovascular risk despite optimal standard therapy.
.
Pharmacodynamic effects

The pharmacodynamics of colchicine in prevention of ischemic cardiovascular events is not completely understood; however, colchicine has been shown to exert cardioprotective, anti-inflammatory and anti-atherosclerotic effects in several animal models in vivo and may stabilize atherosclerotic plaques by reducing inflammatory activity and plaque burden.
Favorable plaque-modifying effects of daily 0.5 mg colchicine therapy, independent of substantial low-density lipoprotein reduction or high-dose statin intensification in patients post-acute coronary syndrome have been observed.
Established pharmacodynamic effect (e.g. effect on uric acid metabolism) does not occur at the recommended posology of this product.

Clinical efficacy and safety

LoDoCo2 (ACTRN12614000093684) was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicentred trial in patients with chronic, stable coronary artery disease (CAD).
A total of 5 522 patients were enrolled, with 2 762 patients assigned to the Colchicine group and 2 760 to the placebo group who underwent median follow-up of 28.6 months.
Patients entered the randomized phase only after completing a 30-day open-label run-in period with low-dose colchicine, during which patients with intolerance to colchicine were excluded from randomisation.
The patients were required to tolerate colchicine during a pre-randomisation run-in period prior to study entry.

Eligible patients were adults with documented coronary artery disease demonstrated by invasive coronary angiography, computed tomography coronary angiography, or a coronary-artery calcium score of at least 400 Agatston units, who had been clinically stable for at least 6 months.
Patients with moderate-to-severe renal impairment, severe heart failure, severe valvular heart disease, or known intolerance to colchicine were excluded.

Baseline characteristics were generally balanced between treatment groups.
The mean age of the study population was approximately 66 years, and the majority of patients were male.
Most participants had established cardiovascular risk factors and were receiving standard secondary prevention therapy, including lipid-lowering agents and antiplatelet therapy.

The primary efficacy endpoint was the time to first occurrence of a composite of cardiovascular death, spontaneous myocardial infarction, ischaemic stroke, or ischaemia-driven coronary revascularisation.

Treatment with colchicine significantly reduced the risk of the primary composite cardiovascular endpoint compared with placebo (hazard ratio [HR] 0.69; 95% confidence interval [CI] 0.57–0.83; p<0.001), corresponding to a relative risk reduction of approximately 31%.

The interpretation of the efficacy results is subject to several limitations.
In LoDoCo2, patients entered the randomised phase only after completing a 30-day open-label run-in period with colchicine, during which patients with early intolerance were excluded.
The study population excluded patients with moderate to severe renal impairment, severe heart failure, and other high-risk conditions, which may limit the applicability of results to these populations.
Event ascertainment focused on prespecified cardiovascular outcomes, and the collection of non-cardiovascular safety data was limited.

Supportive evidence is available from additional randomised studies and meta-analytic data in atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease.
However, results across cardiovascular trials are heterogeneous, particularly between stable coronary disease and acute myocardial infarction populations.
In CLEAR SYNERGY/OASIS-9, a large, randomized trial in patients treated shortly after acute myocardial infarction, colchicine did not significantly reduce the primary composite cardiovascular endpoint.
The interpretation of this study differs from stable CAD trials due to differences in population, timing of initiation after acute MI, background acute interventional therapy, adherence considerations and trial conduct during the COVID-19 pandemic.

The clinical benefit of colchicine has therefore been established primarily in patients with stable coronary artery disease.
