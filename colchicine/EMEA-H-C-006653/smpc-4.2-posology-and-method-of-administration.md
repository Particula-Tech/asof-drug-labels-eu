# 4.2 Posology and method of administration

Posology

Adults
The recommended dose is 0.5 mg of colchicine once daily.
The maximum daily dose is 0.5 mg.
The recommended maximum dose must not be exceeded.
The initial clinical manifestations of such overdose include gastrointestinal disturbances such as diarrhoea, nausea, and vomiting (see section 4.4).
If a dose of colchicine is missed, the missed dose should be taken as soon as possible, and the patient should then return to the normal dosing schedule.
If a dose is skipped, the patient should not double the next dose.

Duration of treatment
Colchicine can be safely used for a period of 28 months.

Elderly
No dose adjustment is necessary.
Clinical studies included elderly patients (≥65 years); however, very elderly patients (≥75 years) were under-represented.
Available evidence does not indicate a clinically relevant age-related difference in safety or efficacy.
As renal impairment, comorbid conditions and concomitant medicines are more common in elderly patients, colchicine should be used with caution in this population.

Renal impairment
No dose adjustment is required in patients with mildly decreased (eGFR 60-89 mL/min) or normal renal function (eGFR ≥90 mL/min).
Patients with mildly to moderately decreased renal function with eGFR ≥50 mL/min should be monitored closely for adverse reactions of colchicine.
However, colchicine is contraindicated in patients with renal impairment (eGFR <50 mL/min) (see section 4.3).

Hepatic impairment
Patients with hepatic impairment should be monitored closely for adverse reactions of colchicine.
Colchicine is contraindicated in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child Pugh C).

Paediatric population
There is no relevant use of colchicine in the paediatric population for the indication of secondary prevention of atherothrombotic events in adult patients with coronary disease stable for at least 6 months.

Method of administration

Oral use.
The tablet should be swallowed with a glass of water.
