# 4.4 Special warnings and precautions for use

Important warning
Colchicine has a narrow therapeutic index, rendering it susceptible to significant risks of severe overdose.
The recommended maximum dose must not be exceeded.
The initial clinical manifestations of such overdose include gastrointestinal disturbances such as diarrhoea, nausea, and vomiting.
Depending on the dose taken, potential multiple organ failure may ensue, affecting various systems including respiratory, cardiovascular, haematologic, and nervous system.
Patients should be made aware of these signs of possible overdosage, which may also occur if interactions are ignored.
In this case, a reduction in dose or discontinuation of treatment should be considered.
Please refer to sections 4.4, 4.5, 4.8 and 4.9 of this SmPC.
The medicinal product must be kept out of reach of others before and after use.

Cardiovascular

The use of colchicine in patients with New York Heart Association (NYHA) Class III or IV heart failure and a left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) of less than 35% is not supported by clinical efficacy and safety data.
Therefore, caution is advised when considering colchicine treatment in this population.
Healthcare professionals should carefully assess the potential benefits and risks on an individual basis.
For information on other populations excluded from the LoDoCo2 study, refer to Section 5.1.

Blood dyscrasias

Colchicine can cause myelosuppression, leukopenia, granulocytopenia, thrombocytopenia, pancytopenia, and aplastic anaemia, which can be life-threatening or fatal (see sections 4.3 and 4.8).
Gastrointestinal symptoms often are the first sign of colchicine toxicity, so new symptoms should prompt an evaluation for toxicity.
Concomitant use of medicinal products that reduce the metabolism of colchicine or the presence of hepatic or renal impairment increases the risk of developing blood dyscrasias.
Use of colchicine in patients with pre-existing blood dyscrasias, renal impairment (eGFR <50 mL/min), or severe hepatic dysfunction, and concomitant use of strong CYP3A4 inhibitors or P-glycoprotein inhibitors is contraindicated (see section 4.3).
Concomitant use of moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors with colchicine should be avoided in patients with risk factors for increased systemic exposure of colchicine (see section 4.5).
Patients with any degree of renal impairment and hepatic impairment should be monitored for colchicine toxicity.

Neuromuscular toxicity

Colchicine can cause neuromuscular toxicity and rhabdomyolysis.
Concomitant use of medicinal products that reduce the metabolism of colchicine or the presence of hepatic or renal impairment increases the risk of developing neuromuscular toxicity.

If a patient develops signs of neuromuscular toxicity, colchicine should be discontinued, other causes should be investigated and treated appropriately.
Concomitant use of colchicine and HMG CoA reductase inhibitors, such as atorvastatin, rosuvastatin, simvastatin and other lipid-lowering medicinal products, gemfibrozil, fibrates and digoxin may potentiate the development of myopathy (see section 4.5).

Patients with any degree of renal impairment should be monitored closely for adverse reactions of colchicine.
Colchicine is known to be excreted renally, and the presence of moderate to severe renal impairment has been associated with colchicine toxicity.
Urinary clearance of colchicine and its metabolites may be decreased in patients with impaired renal function.

Patients with any degree of hepatic impairment should be monitored closely for adverse reactions of colchicine.
Colchicine is metabolized primarily by the liver, and severe hepatic impairment has been associated with colchicine toxicity.
Hepatic clearance may be significantly reduced, and plasma half-life prolonged in patients with chronic hepatic impairment.

Women of childbearing potential / Contraception in males and females

Given the mechanism of action of colchicine (see section 5.3), women of childbearing potential should use a highly effective method of contraception while receiving colchicine and for up to one month after end of treatment.
Male patients should not father a child during colchicine therapy, and up to 3 months after last dose of treatment.
Females using oral contraceptives should be prescribed colchicine with caution and observed for adverse reactions.

Based on non-clinical reproductive toxicity findings and the mechanism of action of colchicine, appropriate contraceptive measures are recommended during treatment (see section 4.6).

Excipient (lactose)

Patients with rare hereditary problems of galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency or glucose-galactose malabsorption should not take this medicine.
