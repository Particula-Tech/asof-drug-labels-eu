# 4.8 Undesirable effects

Summary of the safety profile

Gastrointestinal disorders are the most common adverse reactions with colchicine.
These are often the first signs of toxicity and may indicate that treatment needs to be interrupted.
These adverse reactions include diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain or cramping.

Historical use of colchicine has been reported to cause neuromuscular toxicity, which may present as muscle pain or weakness (see section 4.4).
Serious toxic manifestations associated with colchicine include myelosuppression, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and impairment of renal, hepatic, circulatory, and central nervous systems.

Reporting of suspected adverse reactions

Reporting suspected adverse reactions after authorisation of the medicinal product is important.
It allows continued monitoring of the benefit/risk balance of the medicinal product.
Healthcare professionals are asked to report any suspected adverse reactions via the national reporting system listed in Appendix V.
