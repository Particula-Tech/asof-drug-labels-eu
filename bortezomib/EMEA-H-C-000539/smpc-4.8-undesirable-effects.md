# 4.8 Undesirable effects

Two hundred and fifty six (256) patients received VELCADE for the treatment of multiple myeloma, either as a single agent, or in combination with dexamethasone.
Of these 97% of patients (n=248) experienced an adverse drug reaction (ADR) to VELCADE.
ADRs leading to discontinuation were reported in 17% (n=44) of patients.
The reasons for discontinuation were evenly distributed across the most common types of toxicity and included peripheral neuropathy (4%), thrombocytopenia (4%), diarrhoea (2%), and fatigue (2%).
In the Phase II multiple myeloma studies (n=256), patients with calculated creatinine clearances ≤ 50 ml/minute (N=52) had a higher incidence of serious undesirable effects (60% of patients versus 51% and 41% in the 51-80 and > 80 ml/minute groups) with an increase in cardiac serious effects but none was specifically prominent.
The incidence of serious adverse events (SAEs ) was higher in patients with pre-existing renal problems.
Neutropenia by this analysis was a more frequently reported adverse event (AE) in the lower creatinine clearance group but when analyzed by serum creatinine and absolute neutrophil count no relationship was apparent.
Patients 51-65 years and those in the ≥ 65 year old group had similar occurrences of AEs, SAEs and discontinuations.
Those less than 50 years, a smaller subgroup, had less SAEs and discontinuations particularly in the metabolic and vascular system organ classes (SOCs).
There were too few patients with abnormal liver enzymes (≥ 1.5x upper limit of normal (ULN ) for Aspartate aminotransferase (AST) and Alanine aminotransferase (ALT)) to allow definite conclusions on safety in patients with abnormal liver function.

Tumour lysis syndrome
Because bortezomib is a cytotoxic agent and can rapidly kill malignant plasma cells, the complications of tumour lysis syndrome may occur.
The patients at risk of tumour lysis syndrome are those with high tumour burden prior to treatment.
These patients should be monitored closely and appropriate precautions taken.

Potentially immunocomplex-mediated reactions
Potentially immunocomplex-mediated reactions, such as serum-sickness –type reaction, polyarthritis with rash and proliferative glomerulonephritis have been reported uncommonly.
Bortezomib should be discontinued if severe reactions occur.
